# Apex · Further Reading

Reading lists per phase, plus the sources behind the program's design.

**How to use this.** Nothing here is required — the program is complete without
it. Each phase lists one **start here** (accessible, short, high-yield) and a
small number of deeper sources. Facilitators should read the design section (§7)
before running a cohort.

> Titles and authors are given so you can find them through the county library
> system or your preferred bookseller. Editions and availability change — confirm
> before recommending a specific edition to a cohort.

---

## Phase 0 · Entering the Reef

*Mindset, misconception, and the habit of experimenting anyway.*

**Start here** — Carol S. Dweck, *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*. The
popular treatment of fixed vs. growth mindset. Read the first three chapters; the
rest is elaboration.

- Susan A. Ambrose et al., *How Learning Works: Seven Research-Based Principles for
  Smart Teaching*. Chapter 1 on prior knowledge is the research behind this
  module's whole premise: what learners already believe shapes what they can learn,
  and inaccurate prior knowledge actively blocks new understanding.
- Albert Bandura, "Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change"
  (*Psychological Review*, 1977). The origin of the idea that belief in your
  capability predicts what you attempt. Dense but foundational.
- Anders Ericsson & Robert Pool, *Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise*.
  Why deliberate practice with feedback beats experience alone — the argument for
  running a simulation four times rather than once.

## Phase 1 · Apex Positioning

*Motivational focus, game type, positions vs. interests.*

**Start here** — Roger Fisher & William Ury, *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement
Without Giving In*. The separation of positions from interests, which this phase's
summative assessment is built on.

- Heidi Grant Halvorson & E. Tory Higgins, *Focus: Use Different Ways of Seeing the
  World for Success and Influence*. The accessible treatment of promotion vs.
  prevention focus and the diagnostic this phase uses.
- E. Tory Higgins, "Beyond Pleasure and Pain" (*American Psychologist*, 1997). The
  primary source for regulatory focus theory, if you want the research rather than
  the popularisation.
- Avinash Dixit & Barry Nalebuff, *The Art of Strategy: A Game Theorist's Guide to
  Success in Business and Life*. Game theory for people who don't want the maths —
  the zero-sum vs. collaborative distinction with worked examples.

## Phase 2 · Coral Scaffolding

*Business architecture, iterative planning, help-seeking.*

**Start here** — Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation*.
The visual, iterative alternative to a 40-page plan nobody reads.

- Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup*. Build-measure-learn as the loop the "evolving
  document" is an instance of.
- Steve Blank & Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual*. Considerably more practical
  than Ries on the specific question of evidence: what counts as market validation
  and what doesn't.
- Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers*. Short, cheap, and
  directly useful for the market-analysis half of the summative artifact — most
  founder "validation" is a conversation that couldn't have produced a no.
- On help-seeking: the research literature (Arie Nadler and colleagues on
  autonomy-oriented vs. dependency-oriented help-seeking) is worth a search if the
  distinction between "solve this for me" and "give me the tool" interests you.

## Phase 3 · Navigating the Currents

*Integrative negotiation, BATNA, MESOs, anchoring.*

**Start here** — Deepak Malhotra & Max H. Bazerman, *Negotiation Genius*. The
source of MESOs (Multiple Equivalent Simultaneous Offers) as this phase teaches
them, and the best single book on the material.

- Roger Fisher & William Ury, *Getting to Yes* — again, for BATNA specifically.
- Deepak Malhotra, *Negotiating the Impossible*. Shorter, on the negotiations where
  leverage isn't available and framing has to do the work.
- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Part 4 on anchoring explains why
  Marcus Vane's $48,000 works even on people who know it's a tactic.
- Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*. Reads against *Getting to Yes* in
  places, and the tension is instructive — his tactical empathy and labelling map
  closely onto the paraphrase move this phase scores.

## Phase 4 · Schooling Strategy

*Psychological safety, productive disagreement, conversational repair.*

**Start here** — Amy C. Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization: Creating
Psychological Safety in the Workplace for Learning, Innovation, and Growth*.

- Amy C. Edmondson, "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams"
  (*Administrative Science Quarterly*, 1999). The original study.
- Douglas Stone, Bruce Patton & Sheila Heen, *Difficult Conversations: How to
  Discuss What Matters Most*. The practical companion — how to open the
  conversation Priya Raman's scenario requires.
- Sheila Heen & Douglas Stone, *Thanks for the Feedback*. The receiving end, which
  is the harder half and the one leaders skip.
- Emanuel A. Schegloff, Gail Jefferson & Harvey Sacks, "The Preference for
  Self-Correction in the Organization of Repair in Conversation" (*Language*,
  1977). Where "conversational repair" comes from, if you want the linguistics.
  Technical; the concept survives translation.
- Kim Scott, *Radical Candor*. Useful on the specific failure this phase targets:
  caring personally without challenging directly produces the silence, not safety.

## Phase 5 · The Migration

*Total compensation, operational excellence, the pitch.*

**Start here** — Nancy Duarte, *Resonate: Present Visual Stories that Transform
Audiences*. The structure behind a pitch that moves people rather than informing
them.

- Chip Heath & Dan Heath, *Made to Stick*. Why some ideas survive retelling —
  directly applicable to the capstone.
- Andy Rachleff and the Holloway *Guide to Equity Compensation* (holloway.com) for
  the mechanics of equity, vesting, and what a percentage actually means. Read
  before negotiating with Theo Hanson.
- For 2026 market data: the **Robert Half Salary Guide** and **levels.fyi** are the
  two the program's compensation lesson expects you to cite. Use current-year
  figures; a stale number is worse than no number in a negotiation.
- On operational excellence: Google's *Site Reliability Engineering* (free at
  sre.google/books) is the serious version of the Reef-Native philosophy —
  performance budgets, graceful degradation, designing for constrained
  environments.

## Phase 6 · On AI as a working tool

Cross-cutting; relevant from Phase 0 onward, since every phase asks learners to
attempt things with AI assistance.

- Ethan Mollick, *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI*. The best general
  treatment of working alongside these tools, and honest about the limits.
- Ethan Mollick's newsletter *One Useful Thing* — more current than any book can
  be on a subject that moves this fast.

## §7 · The program's own design

For facilitators, and for anyone extending the curriculum.

**Instructional design**

- John Biggs, "Enhancing Teaching Through Constructive Alignment" (*Higher
  Education*, 1996). Why every objective in the manifests is both taught in a
  lesson and measured by an assessment, with two-way links. This is the integrity
  guarantee the module manifests encode.
- Lorin W. Anderson & David R. Krathwohl (eds.), *A Taxonomy for Learning,
  Teaching, and Assessing*. The revised Bloom's taxonomy behind every objective's
  `bloomLevel` and `actionVerb`.
- Malcolm S. Knowles, *The Adult Learner: The Definitive Classic in Adult Education
  and Human Resource Development*. Andragogy — why this program starts with the
  learner's own venture rather than a case study.

**Experiential learning**

- David A. Kolb, *Experiential Learning: Experience as the Source of Learning and
  Development* (1984). The four-stage cycle the debrief agent walks: Concrete
  Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualization → Active
  Experimentation.
- Graham Gibbs, *Learning by Doing: A Guide to Teaching and Learning Methods*
  (1988). The reflective cycle that adds an explicit "feelings" stage — a
  reasonable swap for the emotion-heavy leadership module. See
  `docs/experiential-learning-design.md` §3.
- Donald Schön, *The Reflective Practitioner*. Reflection-in-action vs.
  reflection-on-action; the distinction between what the simulation does and what
  the debrief does.

**Evaluation**

- Donald L. Kirkpatrick & James D. Kirkpatrick, *Evaluating Training Programs: The
  Four Levels*. The post-program survey is built on this — reaction, learning,
  behavior, results, in that order. It's why the instrument asks about applied
  behaviour months out, not just satisfaction on the day.

## The short list

If you're facilitating and have time for three books:

1. Amy Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization* — you cannot run the paired debriefs
   well without understanding psychological safety.
2. Deepak Malhotra & Max Bazerman, *Negotiation Genius* — two of the six
   simulations are negotiations.
3. Susan Ambrose et al., *How Learning Works* — the research the program's
   "expose misconceptions" premise rests on.
