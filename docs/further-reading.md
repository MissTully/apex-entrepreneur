# Apex · Further Reading & Growth Library

**Version 1.0 · June 2026**

A curated reading list for each module/phase — foundational sources behind the
concepts Apex teaches, plus practitioner works for continued growth. Each entry has
a one-line note on *why it's here* and which objective(s) it deepens.

**How to use this list.** These are starting points, not a syllabus. Facilitators
should assign 1–2 per module as optional depth; motivated learners can self-direct.
Citations are given at the work level (author, title, year, publisher/journal);
**verify current editions and links before distributing**, as editions and page
numbers change. Where a concept traces to a specific paper, the seminal article is
cited alongside the popular treatment.

Format follows the program's design-doc convention:
`Author, A. A. (Year). *Title.* Publisher/Journal.`

## Cross-cutting foundations (the program's spine)

- Kolb, D. A. (1984). *Experiential Learning: Experience as the Source of Learning and Development.* Prentice Hall. — The simulate-then-debrief cycle the whole experiential layer runs on.
- Gibbs, G. (1988). *Learning by Doing: A Guide to Teaching and Learning Methods.* Oxford Polytechnic. — Adds an explicit "feelings" stage; useful for the leadership module's debriefs.
- Fanning, R. M., & Gaba, D. M. (2007). "The Role of Debriefing in Simulation-Based Learning." *Simulation in Healthcare, 2*(2). — Evidence base for why the debrief, not the sim, is where learning happens.
- Biggs, J., & Tang, C. (2011). *Teaching for Quality Learning at University* (4th ed.). Open University Press. — Source of *constructive alignment*, the objective↔assessment integrity Apex enforces.
- Brown, P. C., Roediger, H. L., & McDaniel, M. A. (2014). *Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning.* Belknap/Harvard. — Retrieval, spacing, and desirable difficulty for the async work between sessions.
- Lang, J. M. (2021). *Small Teaching* (2nd ed.). Jossey-Bass. — Practical, evidence-based micro-moves for facilitators.

## Module 0 · Entering the Reef — mindset, belief, and purpose
*Deepens LO1–LO6: belief-as-prediction, experimentation, failure diagnosis, self-judgment, chosen vs. inherited goals, human-values "why."*

**Foundational**
- Dweck, C. S. (2006). *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success.* Random House. — Fixed vs. growth mindset; the core of "ability gap is the most temporary."
- Bandura, A. (1977). "Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change." *Psychological Review, 84*(2). — Why attempting despite disbelief changes capability.
- Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (1985). *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior.* Plenum. — Autonomy, competence, relatedness behind "chosen vs. inherited goals."

**Growth / practitioner**
- Duckworth, A. (2016). *Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance.* Scribner. — The persistence gap.
- Pink, D. H. (2009). *Drive: The Surprising Truth About What Motivates Us.* Riverhead. — Purpose and intrinsic motivation (LO5/LO6).
- Sinek, S. (2009). *Start With Why.* Portfolio. — Articulating an authentic "why."
- Clear, J. (2018). *Atomic Habits.* Avery. — Designing small, low-stakes experiments and identity-based change.

## Module 1 · Apex Positioning — mindset, strategy, motivation & risk
*Deepens LO1–LO3: regulatory focus, game structure, positions vs. interests.*

**Foundational**
- Higgins, E. T. (1997). "Beyond Pleasure and Pain." *American Psychologist, 52*(12). — Regulatory focus theory: promotion vs. prevention.
- Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow.* Farrar, Straus and Giroux. — Loss aversion behind "playing not to lose."
- von Neumann, J., & Morgenstern, O. (1944). *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior.* Princeton University Press. — Origin of the zero-sum vs. cooperative distinction.
- Fisher, R., Ury, W., & Patton, B. (2011). *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In* (3rd ed.). Penguin. — Positions vs. interests (also central to Module 3).

**Growth / practitioner**
- Dixit, A. K., & Nalebuff, B. J. (2008). *The Art of Strategy.* W. W. Norton. — Accessible applied game theory.
- McGrath, R. G., & MacMillan, I. (2000). *The Entrepreneurial Mindset.* Harvard Business School Press. — Strategy and risk under uncertainty.

## Module 2 · Coral Scaffolding — business architecture & iterative planning
*Deepens LO1–LO3: BizChat low-floor/high-ceiling drafting, iterative plan components, help-seeking.*

**Foundational**
- Ries, E. (2011). *The Lean Startup.* Crown Business. — The plan as an evolving, evidence-tested document.
- Blank, S., & Dorf, B. (2012). *The Startup Owner's Manual.* K&S Ranch. — Customer discovery and market evidence.
- Osterwalder, A., & Pigneur, Y. (2010). *Business Model Generation.* Wiley. — Structuring the model before the prose.
- Wood, D., Bruner, J. S., & Ross, G. (1976). "The Role of Tutoring in Problem Solving." *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 17*(2). — The original "scaffolding" the module is named for; low-floor/high-ceiling.

**Growth / practitioner**
- Aulet, B. (2013). *Disciplined Entrepreneurship.* Wiley. — A 24-step build for the iterative plan.
- Maurya, A. (2012). *Running Lean.* O'Reilly. — Lean Canvas; sharpening the riskiest assumption.
- Edmondson, A. C., & Lei, Z. (related work on help-seeking); see also Lee, F. (1997). "When the Going Gets Tough, Do the Tough Ask for Help?" *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 72*(3). — The reputational risk of help-seeking (LO3).

## Module 3 · Navigating the Currents — integrative negotiation & relationship capital
*Deepens LO1–LO3: rapport & BATNA, active listening & MESOs, counter-anchoring.*

**Foundational**
- Fisher, R., Ury, W., & Patton, B. (2011). *Getting to Yes* (3rd ed.). Penguin. — BATNA, interests, objective criteria.
- Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974). "Judgment Under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases." *Science, 185*(4157). — The anchoring effect the counter-anchor neutralizes.
- Malhotra, D., & Bazerman, M. H. (2007). *Negotiation Genius.* Bantam. — MESOs, value creation, and reading the counterpart.

**Growth / practitioner**
- Shell, G. R. (2006). *Bargaining for Advantage* (2nd ed.). Penguin. — A complete integrative-bargaining system.
- Ury, W. (1991). *Getting Past No.* Bantam. — Handling hard tactics and aggressive anchors.
- Voss, C. (2016). *Never Split the Difference.* Harper Business. — Tactical empathy and calibrated questions; pairs with active-listening paraphrase.
- Cialdini, R. B. (2006). *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (rev. ed.). Harper Business. — Reciprocity and the levers underneath rapport.

## Module 4 · Schooling Strategy — leadership dynamics under uncertainty
*Deepens LO1–LO3: psychological safety & productive disagreement, conversational repair, cross-functional coordination.*

**Foundational**
- Edmondson, A. C. (1999). "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams." *Administrative Science Quarterly, 44*(2). — The seminal study behind LO1.
- Edmondson, A. C. (2018). *The Fearless Organization.* Wiley. — Practitioner translation of psychological safety.
- Goleman, D. (1998). "What Makes a Leader?" *Harvard Business Review, 76*(6). — Emotional intelligence in leadership.
- Heifetz, R. A., & Linsky, M. (2002). *Leadership on the Line.* Harvard Business School Press. — Adaptive leadership through disruption.

**Growth / practitioner**
- Lencioni, P. (2002). *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team.* Jossey-Bass. — Trust → productive conflict → commitment.
- Scott, K. (2017). *Radical Candor.* St. Martin's Press. — Care personally, challenge directly; productive disagreement.
- Stone, D., Patton, B., & Heen, S. (2010). *Difficult Conversations* (rev. ed.). Penguin. — The repair toolkit behind conversational-repair strategies.
- Grant, A. (2021). *Think Again.* Viking. — Purposeful inquiry and rethinking under uncertainty.

## Maintaining this list

- Keep it **objective-anchored**: every addition should name the LO it deepens.
- Prefer **one seminal source + one practitioner source** per concept over volume.
- Re-verify editions annually; replace dead links; flag works superseded by newer research.
- When a new module or simulation is authored, add its section here in the same format.
